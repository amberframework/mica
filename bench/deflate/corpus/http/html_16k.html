Before you're ever asked

About the administrator password

The first time you switch HeatPilot into a mode that takes the controls, macOS will show you a HeatPilot-branded administrator prompt. A free app asking for your password is exactly the moment you should get suspicious — so here's the explanation up front, before you ever see it.

HeatPilot is two parts. The menu-bar app — the part you see — is an ordinary, unprivileged app. It reads temperatures and draws menus, and it couldn't touch your fans if it wanted to. Fan hardware is privileged territory, and macOS is right to guard it. So the one job that needs privileges lives in a tiny root helper whose entire vocabulary is “set fan speed” and “hand control back to Apple.” Your password installs that helper, once. That's the whole transaction.

And if you only ever fly in System Auto, you'll never be asked at all.

Apple is always one click away

Built to hand the controls back

  • System Auto restores Apple's thermal management instantly. So does quitting the app. There is no state where HeatPilot is gone and your fans are stranded.
  • The helper watches for kill, logout, and shutdown signals — and its last act is always the same: give Apple back the controls on its way out.
  • Cooler Auto only ever raises fan speed above Apple's own curve. It never slows a fan below what Apple would do. The worst case is a cooler, slightly louder Mac.
  • Fanless Macs and Intel Macs never see a password prompt. The privileged modes simply show as disabled, with a plain explanation of why there's nothing for HeatPilot to fly.

The quiet part, out loud

What HeatPilot never does

No network calls. No account. No telemetry — not “anonymized” telemetry, not “just crash reports.” None. Cut your network and everything still works, because nothing in HeatPilot has anywhere to phone.

Its entire memory of you is a small JSON file in ~/Library/Application Support/HeatPilot/ — your chosen mode and preferences, in plain text you can open and read yourself.

The fine print, kept short

Requirements & uninstall

Requires: an Apple Silicon Mac with fans, running macOS 14 or later. Intel Macs aren't supported — their fan hardware speaks a different language, and we'd rather not support it badly.

Uninstalling leaves nothing behind: switch to System Auto, quit HeatPilot, then delete the app and ~/Library/Application Support/HeatPilot/. Apple has the controls again, as if we were never aboard.
